What I build

Five things, done properly, instead of twenty listed on a page.

Each of these is running somewhere right now for a business that pays for it — not a demo repo with a nice README.

Voice agents on real phone lines

Cloud APIs hand you a SIP endpoint and a webhook. A physical GSM line gives you neither — you terminate the channel yourself, own the audio path in both directions, and write the dialplan that decides what happens on answer and on hangup. I've built both kinds.

  • Asterisk + AGI bridged to a real SIM
  • Vapi, Retell or Twilio when a cloud number is fine
  • Calendar checks and booking inside the call

Pipelines that run unattended

Scrape, filter, route, message, follow up. One process per task, no overlapping runs, working hours enforced.

Systems that touch hardware

Biometric devices over raw TCP, GSM gateways, on-prem installers that register themselves as scheduled tasks. Not everything lives behind a REST API.

CRM and back-office plumbing

HubSpot, GoHighLevel, Airtable, Sheets. The unglamorous part that decides whether the AI sitting on top of it is useful or just expensive.

Full products, not only workflows

Client build · operations

Biometric attendance and penalty engine

An office was reconciling attendance by hand off biometric machines and arguing about break times every month. One service polls the cloud attendance API. A second talks straight to the devices over raw TCP on port 4370, because break minutes are the one thing that API doesn't expose. Both write through a single Apps Script endpoint that applies the fine rules and provisions a fresh tab each day.

How a lead actually moves

Seven steps, no one watching.

01

Scrape

Custom Python pulls target groups and hashtags on a schedule. Nobody opens Facebook.

02

Filter

Most posts in a construction group aren't purchase intent. This step decides whether the system is useful or just annoying.

03

Record

Every qualified lead lands in Sheets with its source post, so the team can audit where anything came from.

04

Message

WhatsApp sends the intro. For this audience, in this market, email is effectively dead.

05

Call

A voice agent phones the lead, has a real conversation, works out what they need, and records the outcome.

06

Quote

Live pricing rules turn a requirement into a real quotation. Nobody opens a spreadsheet.

07

Never at 2am

Contact is locked to weekdays, 9am to 7pm. An automation that texts a stranger in the middle of the night doesn't read as clever. It reads as a scam, and it burns the client's name faster than the pipeline can build it.

How we'd work

You see it running before you pay in full.

Four stages. You can stop after any of them.

01

Audit

I map what you do now and find where the manual work actually is. Usually it isn't where people think — it sits in the handoffs between tools, not inside them.

02

Blueprint

Workflow, data flow, edge cases, and what happens when something fails. Written down before anything gets built, because failure handling is the part that takes the time.

03

Build

Staged, so you see a working piece early and decide whether to keep going. Tested against real scenarios, not the one clean path that demos well.

04

Handoff

Documentation, access, and a walkthrough. If you can't explain your own system to your team, I did the job badly.
